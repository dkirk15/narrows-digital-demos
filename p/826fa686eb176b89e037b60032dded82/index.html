Client site launch checklist

Use this when a Narrows demo is ready to become the client's real website. Plain-English version for someone who doesn't build websites day to day.

Narrows Digital · reusable for every client · fill in blanks before go-live

0 Client snapshot

Fill this in once so nothing depends on memory.

  • Practice / business name: ______________________________
  • Domain (web address): ______________________________
  • Where the domain is registered (e.g. Network Solutions, GoDaddy): ________________
  • Who has login access to the domain account: ________________
  • Do they use email at this domain (@theirbusiness.com)? ☐ Yes ☐ No ☐ Not sure
  • Demo URL: ______________________________
  • Launch date target: ________________

1 Domain (the address)

The domain is rented yearly. It should stay in the practice's name, not yours.

  • Confirm the practice owns the domain (and it isn't about to expire)Stadium already owns theirs through 2035 — still confirm login access.
  • Get into the registrar account (they log in, or you get temporary access)This is often the slowest step for small offices.
  • Write down where renewals are billed and who gets the renewal emails

2 Hosting (where the site lives)

Hosting is a computer that stays online and serves the site files. The demo host is not the client's permanent home.

  • Pick a host (Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, GitHub Pages, or similar — fine for simple sites)
  • Create the hosting account in the client's name (or yours if you're managing it under a clear agreement)
  • Upload / publish the approved site files to that host
  • Store login credentials somewhere the client can recover them later

3 DNS (point the address at the new host)

DNS is the internet's phone book. Changing it is what makes stadiumdentalhealth.com show the new site.

Watch out: If they use @theirbusiness.com email, email settings live in the same DNS area. Change only the website records. Leave MX / email records alone unless an email expert is helping.
  • Screenshot the current DNS settings before changing anything
  • Update the website DNS (usually A / CNAME / ALIAS per the host's instructions)
  • Confirm MX / email records were not touched (if they use domain email)
  • Wait for DNS to settle (minutes to a few hours; sometimes up to 24–48)

4 HTTPS (the padlock)

Browsers expect a secure connection. Most modern hosts turn this on for free.

  • HTTPS / SSL is active on the live domain (padlock shows, no security warning)
  • http:// automatically redirects to https://
  • www and non-www both work and land on the same site

5 Content that must be real

A demo can be approximate. A live site cannot.

  • Practice name, address, phone, hours — exact and approved
  • Doctors / team — correct names and photos (fix demo gaps before launch)
  • Services, insurance, and any claims — approved by the office
  • Photos you have permission to use (no stock that looks fake; no stolen images)
  • Client signed off on the final copy ("this is ready to go live")

6 Booking & contact

Forms have to deliver somewhere. Patients may type personal details, so keep forms simple or use a dental scheduling tool.

  • Decide: phone-only, email form, or real online booking
  • If a form: test that submissions arrive in the right inbox
  • If online booking: connect their scheduling product and test a fake appointment
  • Keep health details out of simple web forms (name, phone, preferred time is enough)

7 Legal & trust basics

  • Privacy policy page (even a short one)
  • Accessibility statement / basic accessible markup (good contrast, readable text, alt text)
  • Footer copyright / business name correct
  • No fake reviews, rankings, or HIPAA claims you can't support

8 Google & discovery

  • Update Google Business Profile website URL to the live domain
  • Hours, phone, and address on Google match the site
  • Optional: set up basic analytics (only if the client wants it and privacy notice covers it)

9 Ownership & business agreement

Decide this before flip day so nobody is stuck later.

  • Domain stays in the client's name
  • Hosting: client-owned vs Narrows-managed (written down)
  • Who can edit the site after launch
  • Price for build / launch / monthly care (ties to your Narrows pricing)
  • What happens if they leave (export of files + how to point DNS elsewhere)

10 Go-live day smoke test

Do this on your phone and a computer, on Wi‑Fi and cellular if you can.

  • Homepage loads on phone and desktop
  • Padlock is good; no certificate warnings
  • Main menu links work
  • Contact / book button works
  • Phone number is tappable on mobile
  • Request a friend outside the office to open the URL once

11 After launch (first week)

  • Watch for "site down" or email-broken reports for 48 hours
  • Save a backup of the live files
  • Calendar a domain renewal reminder (yearly)
  • Optional: redirect any important old pages people still bookmark
  • Send the client a short "you're live" note with the logins and who to call for edits
